Free and open source for Windows 10 and 11

A little black pill at the top of your screen that opens into music, lyrics, your phone, your AI agents and your day. Hover in, get what you need, move on.

Download for Windows View the source

  1. It waits as a pill.

    Compact, quiet, flush with the top of your screen. It shows a time, what's playing, or how much of your AI limit is left. It hides behind full screen windows.

  2. Music, with karaoke lyrics.

    Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube in a browser, anything that talks to Windows. The lyric line fills as it's sung. Covers can spin like vinyl.

  3. Throw files at your phone.

    Drag anything onto the notch and pick it up on your phone over your own Wi-Fi. Approve a phone once and it remembers. No app, no cloud.

  4. Your AI limits, at a glance.

    Codex and Claude Code session and weekly limits, as the providers report them. Turn off what you don't use.

  5. It taps you when it matters.

    An agent finishes, a reminder is due, a code arrives by email. The notch stretches into a live alert, then tucks itself away.

  6. Your whole day, one hover away.

    Weather, calendar, reminders, Bluetooth quick swap, quick settings. Drag widgets into the pages you want.

Questions people ask

Is it really free?

Yes. No account, no premium tier, no ads. It's MIT licensed, so it stays that way.

Does it use my microphone?

No. The visualizer reads what your speakers play. The microphone is never requested.

Does Beam work with an iPhone?

Yes. Scan the code in Safari once, approve the phone on your PC, and bookmark the link Beam shows. After that the phone opens Beam from the bookmark while you're on the same Wi-Fi.

Which music apps work?

Anything that reports to Windows media controls: Spotify, Apple Music, browsers, VLC and most others. Lyrics come from LRCLIB.

Can it read my Windows notifications?

Only after you install the signed package and allow notification access in Windows. Nothing is connected by default.

How heavy is it?

It uses the WebView Windows already ships rather than bundling a browser. Collapsed it's light; open it's a full page, so it uses more memory while open.
